    <h2>Principles behind the method</h2>

    <p>
      PQuest is built around a set of principles that shape how psychological
      skills are practiced.
    </p>

    <h3>Active practice</h3>
    <p>
      Skills are practiced through active engagement rather than passive
      exposure to information. The learner takes part in the experience and
      makes meaningful decisions.<sup><a href="{{ '/method/evidence/#ref-3' | relative_url }}">[3]</a></sup>
    </p>

    <h3>Context matters</h3>
    <p>
      The same response may not work equally well in every situation. PQuest
      places emphasis on understanding circumstances, goals, and possible
      trade-offs rather than applying fixed rules.
    </p>

    <h3>Adaptation over fixed answers</h3>
    <p>
      Situations can change. New information can matter. PQuest encourages
      practice in reconsidering a situation and adapting a response rather than
      searching for one universally “correct” answer.<sup><a href="{{ '/method/evidence/#ref-4' | relative_url }}">[4]</a></sup>
    </p>

    <h3>Learning through consequences</h3>
    <p>
      Decisions become meaningful when their possible consequences can be
      considered. PQuest uses consequences as part of the practice experience,
      helping make relationships between situations, actions, and outcomes
      visible.
    </p>

    <h3>Feedback without judgment</h3>
    <p>
      Feedback is intended to support reflection on situations, decisions, and
      consequences — not to label the person or reduce the experience to simply
      being “right” or “wrong.”<sup><a href="{{ '/method/evidence/#ref-5' | relative_url }}">[5]</a></sup>
    </p>

    <p>
      Together, these principles support a form of practice designed around
      engagement, context, reflection, and adaptation.
    </p>
