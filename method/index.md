      <h1>The PQuest Method</h1>
      <p class="lead">A psychological skills training method.</p>

      <p>
        Psychological skills are not only something we can understand — they are
        something we can practice.
      </p>

      <p>
        The PQuest Method provides a framework for turning psychological skills
        into active, contextual practice. It is designed around experiences in
        which people engage with situations, make decisions, encounter changing
        conditions, and adapt their responses.
      </p>

      <p>
        Rather than teaching a single “right” way to respond, PQuest focuses on
        practicing how to navigate variation, uncertainty, and trade-offs across
        different contexts.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

      <h2>Practice before it matters.</h2>

      <p>
        Knowledge is essential, but knowing what to do is not always the same as
        being able to use that knowledge when a situation unfolds.
      </p>

      <p>
        In fields such as healthcare and aviation, simulation- and scenario-based
        training create opportunities to practice skills and decisions in realistic
        contexts before they are needed in real-world situations.<sup>[1,2]</sup>
      </p>

      <p>
        PQuest builds on the broader principle behind this approach: practice
        should not begin only when the real situation arrives.
      </p>

      <p>
        The PQuest Method brings active, contextual practice to psychological
        skills — creating opportunities to engage with situations, make decisions,
        encounter change, and adapt within the training environment.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">

      <h2>Principles behind the method</h2>

      <p>
        PQuest is built around a set of principles that shape how psychological
        skills are practiced.
      </p>

      <h3>Active practice</h3>
      <p>
        Skills are practiced through active engagement rather than passive
        exposure to information. The learner takes part in the experience and
        makes meaningful decisions.<sup>[3]</sup>
      </p>

      <h3>Context matters</h3>
      <p>
        The same response may not work equally well in every situation. PQuest
        places emphasis on understanding circumstances, goals, and possible
        trade-offs rather than applying fixed rules.
      </p>

      <h3>Adaptation over fixed answers</h3>
      <p>
        Situations can change. New information can matter. PQuest encourages
        practice in reconsidering a situation and adapting a response rather than
        searching for one universally “correct” answer.<sup>[4]</sup>
      </p>

      <h3>Learning through consequences</h3>
      <p>
        Decisions become meaningful when their possible consequences can be
        considered. PQuest uses consequences as part of the practice experience,
        helping make relationships between situations, actions, and outcomes
        visible.
      </p>

      <h3>Feedback without judgment</h3>
      <p>
        Feedback is intended to support reflection on situations, decisions, and
        consequences — not to label the person or reduce the experience to simply
        being “right” or “wrong.”<sup>[5]</sup>
      </p>

      <p>
        Together, these principles support a form of practice designed around
        engagement, context, reflection, and adaptation.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
