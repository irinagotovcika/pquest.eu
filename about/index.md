      <h1>About PQuest</h1>

      <p class="lead">
        PQuest is an independent project conceived and developed by Irina Gotovcika.
      </p>

      <p>
        PQuest explores how psychological skills can be supported through active,
        contextual training experiences. The project brings together psychological
        theory, methodological design, and digital technology in the development of
        evidence-informed training tools.
      </p>

      <p>
        PQuest is currently developed as an independent professional project by its creator.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-muted">
  <div class="container">
    <div class="text-container">
      <h2>Project status</h2>

      <p>
        PQuest is an evolving project. The PQuest Method provides the general framework,
        while individual PQuest Trainings apply the method to specific psychological
        skills and contexts.
      </p>

      <p>
        PQuest Brave is the first implementation of this approach. It focuses on social
        self-efficacy and is currently under development.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="container">
    <div class="text-container">
      <h2>Evidence &amp; research status</h2>

      <p>
        PQuest is developed as an evidence-informed project. Its theoretical and
        methodological foundations draw on relevant psychological research, while
        the PQuest Method itself has not yet been empirically validated.
      </p>

      <p>
        Research relating specifically to PQuest and individual PQuest Trainings will
        be distinguished from the literature supporting their theoretical foundations.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-muted">
  <div class="container">
    <div class="text-container">
      <h2>Development approach</h2>

      <p>
        PQuest is conceived, designed, and developed by Irina Gotovcika. AI-based tools
        are used as supporting tools during parts of the development process, including
        research assistance, content review, and language-related tasks.
      </p>

      <p>
        The use of these tools does not replace human responsibility for the project's
        methodological decisions, content, or scientific claims.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
